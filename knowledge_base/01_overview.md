# Overview and Classification of Soil-Erosion Control Methods

## What is soil erosion
Soil erosion is the detachment and transport of soil particles from one place to another by agents such as water, wind, ice or gravity. In agriculture the two most important agents are water and wind. Water erosion occurs as splash (raindrop impact), sheet, rill and gully erosion, and stream-bank erosion. Wind erosion occurs mainly by saltation (bouncing of sand-sized particles), suspension (fine dust carried high in the air) and surface creep (rolling of larger particles). Erosion removes the fertile topsoil that holds most organic matter and nutrients, lowers crop productivity, and causes off-site problems such as sedimentation of reservoirs, canals and rivers.

## Why erosion happens (causes relevant to control)
Erosion is accelerated when the soil surface is left bare, when slopes are long and steep, when water is allowed to flow downslope with high velocity, when soil structure is weak (low organic matter, over-tillage, compaction), and when strong winds blow across dry, loose, bare, smooth fields. Common human causes include deforestation, overgrazing, up-and-down-slope cultivation, excessive tillage, burning or removal of crop residues, and poor drainage of runoff. Almost every control technique works by addressing one or more of these causes: protecting the surface, slowing water or wind, shortening slope length, or improving the soil's resistance.

## Major techniques and methods of controlling soil erosion (classification of soil conservation methods)
Soil-erosion control (soil conservation) methods are commonly classified into three main groups, plus general soil-management practices:

1. **Agronomic / crop-management measures** – use crops, cropping systems and soil-surface management to protect the soil. Examples: crop rotation, strip cropping, cover cropping, contour farming, conservation tillage, reduced tillage, no-till farming, mulching, intercropping and crop-residue management.
2. **Vegetative / biological measures** – use permanent or semi-permanent vegetation such as trees, shrubs and grasses. Examples: afforestation/reforestation, grass cover, vegetative barriers, shelterbelts, windbreaks, agroforestry, pasture/grassland management and riparian buffer strips.
3. **Mechanical / engineering measures** – use earthworks and structures to control the movement of water. Examples: contour bunding, graded bunding, terracing (including bench terraces), contour trenches, check dams, gully plugging, grassed waterways, diversion drains, water-harvesting structures and sediment traps.
4. **General soil-management practices** – maintain the soil's own resistance to erosion: organic-matter management, compost/manure application, conservation agriculture, maintaining soil structure, controlled grazing, avoiding excessive tillage and maintaining ground cover.

Methods can also be grouped by the type of erosion they target: **water-erosion control** (e.g. contour farming, bunding, terracing, mulching, cover crops, check dams, gully plugging, grassed waterways, runoff management) and **wind-erosion control** (e.g. windbreaks, shelterbelts, vegetative cover, crop residues, strip cropping, mulching, afforestation, reducing bare soil). In practice the most effective programmes combine agronomic, vegetative and mechanical measures, usually on a watershed basis.

## Basic principles behind erosion control
All soil-conservation techniques rely on a few basic principles:
- **Protect the soil surface** from raindrop impact and wind by keeping it covered with living plants, residues or mulch.
- **Reduce the velocity and volume of runoff** by increasing infiltration, shortening slope length and reducing effective slope (contour cultivation, bunds, terraces).
- **Dispose of excess runoff safely** through stable, protected channels (grassed waterways, diversion drains, outlets).
- **Reduce wind velocity at the ground surface** with barriers (windbreaks, shelterbelts) and surface roughness (residues, ridges, strip crops).
- **Improve soil structure and aggregate stability** through organic matter, minimal disturbance and good grazing management, so the soil resists detachment.
- **Trap eroded sediment** before it leaves the field (vegetative barriers, check dams, sediment traps, buffer strips).
