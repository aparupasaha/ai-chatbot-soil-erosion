# D. Water-Erosion Control

## Methods that control water erosion - overview
Water erosion is caused by raindrop impact (splash) and by runoff flowing over the land (sheet, rill and gully erosion). Water-erosion control methods therefore aim to (1) protect the soil surface from raindrops, (2) increase infiltration so less water runs off, (3) reduce the velocity of runoff by shortening and flattening slopes, and (4) carry excess runoff away safely through stable channels. The major practices are:
- **Contour farming** – rows and furrows across the slope act as small barriers that slow runoff and hold water.
- **Contour bunding** – level earthen bunds on the contour store runoff and divide long slopes (low rainfall, permeable soils).
- **Graded bunding** – bunds with a slight gradient that carry excess runoff safely to an outlet (higher rainfall, less permeable soils).
- **Terracing / bench terracing** – reduces both slope length and steepness on sloping and hilly land.
- **Mulching** – a surface layer that absorbs raindrop energy, prevents crusting and slows runoff.
- **Vegetative cover** – crop canopy, grass and trees intercept rain and roots bind soil.
- **Cover crops** – keep the soil covered between main crops.
- **Crop-residue management and conservation tillage** – surface residues protect the soil and improve infiltration.
- **Check dams and gully plugging** – slow water in gullies, trap sediment and stop gully growth.
- **Grassed waterways and diversion drains** – safe disposal of concentrated runoff.
- **Runoff management and water harvesting** – collecting, diverting or storing runoff so it does not cause erosion.
- **Vegetative barriers, buffer strips and sediment traps** – filter runoff and trap sediment.
In practice these are combined on a watershed basis: treatment starts from the ridge (upper catchment) with vegetation and trenches, continues on cropland with agronomic practices, bunds and terraces, and ends in the drainage lines with waterways, check dams and gully plugs.

## Runoff management
**Definition:** Runoff management is the planned control of the surface water that does not infiltrate during rainfall – reducing its amount, slowing it down, and conveying it safely – so that it does not cause erosion or flooding.
**How it works:** It combines three strategies: (1) increase infiltration and storage on the land (vegetative cover, mulching, conservation tillage, contour farming, contour bunds, trenches); (2) reduce runoff velocity by shortening slopes (bunds, terraces, strip cropping, vegetative barriers); (3) safely collect and dispose of excess water (diversion drains, graded bunds, grassed waterways, drop structures, check dams) or harvest it (farm ponds, percolation tanks).
**How it reduces erosion:** The erosive power of flowing water increases sharply with its velocity and depth; keeping runoff small, slow and spread out prevents rill and gully formation, and safe outlets prevent concentrated flows from cutting channels.
**Considerations:** Must be planned for the whole catchment/watershed, because water from upslope areas affects lower fields; structures need outlets designed for expected peak flows; regular maintenance is essential.

## Vegetative cover for water-erosion control
Keeping a living plant cover (crops, grasses, trees) on the soil is one of the most important ways of controlling water erosion. The canopy intercepts raindrops and absorbs their energy so they cannot splash soil particles; plant litter and stems slow down overland flow and trap sediment; roots bind the soil and create channels that increase infiltration, which reduces runoff volume; and organic matter from plants improves soil aggregate stability. Erosion is greatest when soil is bare, for example after ploughing, after harvest, or on overgrazed land, so practices that keep cover throughout the year (cover crops, residues, grass, trees, controlled grazing) are central to water-erosion control.
